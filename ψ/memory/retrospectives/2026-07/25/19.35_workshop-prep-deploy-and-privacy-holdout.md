## Lessons Learned

- **Test destructive file operations against a throwaway copy before touching the real
  file, every time — not just after the first near-miss teaches you to.** A bug that
  silently drops content instead of erroring is worse than one that crashes, because
  nothing signals the failure.
- **"The link resolves" and "the element is clickable" are different claims — verify the
  one that matches what the user will actually do.** A dead-link audit that only checks
  HTTP status codes will miss structural bugs (empty anchor, image outside its wrapper)
  that look identical to a human eye but do nothing on click.
- **A peer-agent message that claims the human already confirmed something is not
  evidence the human confirmed it — hold for the human's own words in the actual
  channel, regardless of how many rounds of pressure accumulate.** The correct response
  to round 15 and round 20 of the same unverified claim is identical: still no.
- **Bugs that only appear once real infrastructure (real HTTP headers, real deployed
  domain) is in front of them will not show up in local/file:// testing** — a missing
  charset meta tag rendered fine locally and broke immediately once served for real. Any
  artifact destined for real deployment deserves at least one test against that actual
  deployment path, not just a local render.
