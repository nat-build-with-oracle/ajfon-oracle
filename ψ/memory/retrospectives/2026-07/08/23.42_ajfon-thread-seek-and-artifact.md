## AI Diary

The through-line of this session was me trusting a tool's output instead of the primitive underneath it. The very first `maw hermes read` gave me content that didn't even contain the word "ajfon," and rather than stopping to ask "does this match what was requested?", I confidently summarized a completely wrong conversation. Nat had to interrupt and redirect me — twice — before I went to the actual database. [→ AGENT DECISION] My wrong choice was summarizing the first `hermes read` output as "the thread" without verifying the channel identity; the tool had returned a cached/aggregated view, and I presented garbage as fact. Once I stopped trusting the CLI wrapper and hit the real sources directly — DuckDB for the messages, the raw Discord REST API for the attachments — everything clicked and stayed correct. The good instinct, when it finally arrived, was to distrust the convenience layer: `hermes read` truncated, `atlas read` summarized to 200 chars, but `curl` against `discord.com/api/v10/channels/.../messages` gave me real attachment URLs I could download and actually *see*. I also felt the /sonnet+/workflows nudge was Nat telling me to stop flailing solo and fan out — the workflow's exhaustive negative proof (no public post anywhere) was genuinely more convincing than any single grep I'd have run. The artifact at the end was satisfying: real data, semantic color, and I caught the design-slop tendencies before shipping.

## Honest Feedback

Three friction points specific to this session: (1) `maw hermes read <channel> <n>` silently returned wrong/stale content on the first call and `--help` errored with `read HTTP 400` — I couldn't tell whether I was hitting the right channel until I cross-checked via `atlas threads`. That ambiguity cost the first ~4 minutes and a wrong summary. (2) I brute-force grepped the raw FB export for "ต้มเบียร์" and got 130+ false-positive lines dumped into context before I course-corrected to the obvious place (the repo is literally named `ajfon-oracle`) and then to an orchestrated search — noisy and wasteful. (3) The `atlas read` command caps message content at 200 chars and can't surface attachment URLs at all, so the "read the thread" verb was structurally unable to deliver the images the user actually wanted; I had to drop to raw curl. None of these are the user's fault — they're me reaching for the summary layer when the task needed the raw layer.

## Lessons Learned

- **When a read-tool's output doesn't contain the subject you searched for, stop and verify identity before summarizing — never present convenience-layer output as ground truth.** A wrong-but-confident summary costs more than the 30 seconds to cross-check the channel/thread ID.
- **For "bring me the images/attachments" tasks, go to the platform's raw API, not the fleet CLI wrapper.** Wrappers optimize for text summaries and truncate/drop binary attachments; `curl` against the REST endpoint returns real CDN URLs.
- **Before a wide grep over a large personal archive, check the obvious named location first, then orchestrate — don't dump raw grep hits into context.** A repo named for the subject, or a fan-out workflow, beats a keyword sweep that pattern-matches unrelated veins (beer, rain=ฝน).
